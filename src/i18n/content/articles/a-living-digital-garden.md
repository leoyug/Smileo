---
title: "How I tend a growing digital garden"
description: "Choosing an order for photos, leaving uncertain information blank, and allowing room to undo changes. I tend one small patch at a time."
category: "Personal website"
---
My website, Smileo, holds 99 photographs, along with articles and projects. Some are ready to share, others remain drafts. One article, A home on the internet, has a title and not a single word of body text.

Looking at the directory makes me want to tidy it again. Once I start, though, I hesitate. What exactly am I trying to change? Is something hard to find, or does it simply look untidy? I do not want every visit to my own site to begin with another round of assignments.

So this is about how I give these things a place, and where I plan to go easier on myself.

## I want to choose the order

I separated uploading photos from arranging them. New uploads appear first in the editor so they are easy to find, but I still want to choose their order on the website.

If I rediscover an old photograph tomorrow, I may not want it at the front. It might look better beside another picture, or I might simply want to move a recent favorite forward. I scroll through the photography page myself, and I want that experience to have some interest.

If every photograph needed a complete rationale for its entrance, though, I might never upload the hundredth one. For now, I put it in and see whether it sits comfortably beside its neighbors. If one passage feels awkward, I adjust that passage rather than demanding a perfect order all at once.

I do not know whether I will keep doing this. A larger collection might make it feel like too much work. I can change it then.

## Leave what I cannot remember blank

I made room for dates, places and equipment details, but those fields being there does not mean they all need filling.

If I remember where a photo was taken but not the exact date, I first look for evidence in the old files. If I find it, I add it. Otherwise, I leave the field blank. What matters more is that, when I return later, I can distinguish what was certain from what I was unsure about at the time.

As a designer, I naturally want a page to feel complete and look good. While entering content, I have to resist that impulse a little. A missing line still shows me what is missing. If I casually guess, I may later forget that it was a guess.

Projects need particular care. Some already have covers and look convincing in a list, but opening them reveals responsibilities, processes and images that still need checking. I am willing to leave them as drafts, so pleasant typography does not persuade me that the story is finished too.

## I need to be able to change my mind

I cannot guarantee that every choice will be right. If several photos are similar, I might delete one, then decide I preferred the previous sequence. Even imagining that makes me want a way to retrieve it.

So I leave some room for adjustment. Imported content starts as a draft and becomes public after checking. Deleted entries are temporarily kept on my computer and can be restored manually. Seeing the changed page makes it easier to decide whether I actually like it.

Still, local copies need looking after, and restoration is not automatic. This safety margin is mainly there to help me take action; it should not become an excuse to keep everything forever. I do still want to tidy the garden.

The arrangement I can accept for now is to handle one concrete thing each time I return. After moving a few photos or checking an article, I can close it. I know other areas remain untidy. I can come back to them.

As for A home on the internet, I still have not decided what belongs under that title. I will leave it there for now. I am curious to see what it becomes when I finally write it.
