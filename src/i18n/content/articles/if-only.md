---
title: "I keep thinking: if only I had done things differently"
description: "Looking back with today’s knowledge, the futures omitted from our imagined alternatives, and continuing to choose while living with regret."
---
Long after a conversation ends, I can always think of what I should have said. The tone is right, the logic complete, my meaning clear without making things awkward. I have even prepared the other person's possible replies.

Unfortunately, everyone has already left.

Small things work this way, and larger things are similar. If only I had persisted, spoken sooner, not agreed, asked one more question. Something I rarely think about can resurface through a small reminder and keep me looking backward, as if changing one short passage would resolve everything unsatisfying afterward.

I know I cannot return. Knowing that and stopping the thoughts do not seem to have an automatic connection.

## Usually, only I go back

When I imagine doing it again, the person I send back usually carries everything I know now: which words will hurt, which opportunity will disappear, and which fear that once kept me awake will never happen.

Choosing with that information is easier. Forget what happened later and leave only what was visible then, however, and I am less sure whether I would choose differently.

I might think I should have explained myself clearly. Today it seems like a few sentences and a conversation. Back in that situation, I would have to bear the awkwardness, without knowing whether the other person would accept it. I might not even have understood what I wanted. What I can express smoothly now may then have been only an uncomfortable feeling.

There were also times when I knew what to do: I should respond but kept postponing, or knew a remark was excessive and said it anyway. I do not believe every problem can be explained by having had no choice.

I want to remember more about why I did not manage it. Was I avoiding conflict, fearing rejection, or assuming delay would be harmless? Understanding that gives me more than asking why I was so stupid. Self-criticism can feel satisfying, but the same fear will still be there next time.

## I rarely imagine what comes after if only

The sentence if only I had done that usually ends at things would have been better. I rarely think carefully about what follows.

Suppose I had been braver and said what I thought. Then what? In my imagined version, the other person is generally cooperative, understands and gives a response I can accept. Even our disagreements are more dignified than real ones. Mental rehearsal does make the scene easier to control.

They might also understand perfectly and still refuse. A clear conversation does not necessarily deliver what I want. It may simply reveal sooner that something will not work.

This does not prove that my original choice was better. Another outcome could have been much better; some missed opportunities genuinely have no equivalent replacement. I do not want to comfort myself by insisting that all choices are alike.

I simply tend to imagine the path I did not take as too smooth. My actual life retains the awkward moments and later difficulties. The alternative can stop at its most satisfying point. Put them side by side and the life in front of me has a hard time winning.

Imagination can be pleasant. I can finish saying what I left unsaid. But if I begin seriously disliking my own life, I should ask how much I have omitted from the other side of the comparison.

## Some regrets do not need a happy explanation

I dislike the demand that regret must eventually conclude with fortunately it happened that way. As if a mistake needs to have produced something better before I am allowed to leave it behind.

Some things were simply handled badly. I could have been more attentive; some hurtful words did not need saying. Understanding that later does not let me repair the past. Learning from it is useful, but given a choice, I might still prefer that it had not happened.

That can coexist with enjoying my life now.

I can like the present and still find a part of the past regrettable. Protecting today does not require calling every earlier decision correct. Otherwise, even admitting I was wrong becomes difficult, as if that would remove the foundation from all the years afterward.

Some regrets offer little to summarize. Nobody seriously thought it was the last chance at the time. Circumstances changed, and something once ordinary became hard to arrange. Remembering it naturally hurts.

I want to let that sadness stay for a while. If I do not yet know how to think about it, I can leave it there without rushing to cover it with a lesson. The harder I try to persuade myself I do not care, the more my mind sometimes has to say about it.

## I still have choices to make now

One part of regret makes me wary. To prove an earlier choice was right, I might continue something I no longer want. After spending so long, how can I admit it does not fit? If I change my mind after insisting so strongly, does that make my old self ridiculous?

A decision that only needed to explain the past gradually starts governing today.

I would rather reconsider today's conditions. Do I still want to continue? Can I change things? Am I willing to carry the difficulties that change would bring? My answers can differ from before. I know things I did not know then, and my interests may have changed.

If I regret words I never said, I can ask whether saying them now is appropriate. Some relationships can restart. Some apologies may no longer be needed by the other person. Wanting relief for myself does not mean they must participate again. What can be repaired depends on the situation now.

I will probably continue thinking if only. When the present is difficult, an old turning point can suddenly seem terribly important, as if all today's unhappiness were waiting there for an explanation.

In a while, though, I may look back and criticize today too. How did I miss something so obvious? Why did I not say something so simple? Why was I hesitating when there was still a chance?

I can complain then. The person here today does not even know what tomorrow will bring.
